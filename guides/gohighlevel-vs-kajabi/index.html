GoHighLevel vs Kajabi: Course Platforms and Marketing Tools Compared for Founders (2026)

GoHighLevel vs Kajabi compared for course creators, coaches, and online educators. Pricing, course hosting, funnels, email, community, CRM, and which platform fits your business model.

If you sell knowledge — courses, coaching programs, memberships, digital products — you have probably looked at both of these platforms and walked away confused. Kajabi is the darling of the course creator world. GoHighLevel is the platform agencies and service businesses swear by. On the surface, they look like entirely different categories. Dig deeper, and you find surprising overlap: both build funnels, both send email, both host courses, both process payments. The question is which one fits the way you actually run your business.

This is not a simple comparison because these platforms were built for different founders with different priorities. Kajabi was designed from day one for creators who want to package expertise into digital products and sell them with a polished, premium experience. GoHighLevel was built for agencies and service businesses that need CRM, automation, SMS, and client management — and later added course hosting to the stack. That origin story shapes everything about how they work today.

Quick Verdict

Choose GoHighLevel if you run a service business, agency, or coaching practice that needs CRM, SMS, pipeline management, and appointment booking alongside courses. You get unlimited contacts and courses for $97/month with no per-product caps.

Choose Kajabi if your entire business is built around selling courses and memberships, you want the most polished student experience available, and you do not need CRM, SMS marketing, or outbound sales tools. Kajabi's course delivery and content experience are genuinely best-in-class.

What Kajabi Does Well

Kajabi deserves its reputation. For pure course creators and digital product sellers, it remains one of the most refined platforms available. Understanding its genuine strengths is important before comparing it to anything else.

Best-in-Class Course Experience. Kajabi's course builder produces a student-facing experience that looks and feels like a premium product. Video lessons load cleanly, progress tracking is intuitive, and the overall interface communicates quality without any custom design work. Drip scheduling, prerequisite gating, and completion tracking are all native. Students get a dashboard showing their enrolled products, progress, and upcoming content. For a creator selling a $997 course, this polish matters — it reinforces the perceived value of the product.

Native Assessments and Certificates. Kajabi includes quizzes, surveys, and completion certificates directly in the course builder. This is not a trivial feature. Certification programs, professional development courses, and coaching programs with accountability checkpoints all depend on these tools. GoHighLevel's membership builder does not offer native assessments.

Community Platform. Kajabi's community feature has matured into a genuinely useful product. Discussion threads, member challenges, scheduled events, and a dedicated mobile app create an experience that sits somewhere between a Facebook Group and Circle. For membership businesses where community engagement directly correlates to retention, this is a meaningful advantage.

Polished Checkout and Product Delivery. Kajabi's checkout pages are clean, conversion-optimized, and support one-click upsells, order bumps, free trials, and payment plans natively. The moment a customer purchases, they immediately land inside the product with their account created. The handoff from marketing to delivery is seamless. No Zapier. No manual provisioning.

Podcasting Built In. Kajabi can host and distribute your podcast to Apple Podcasts, Spotify, and other directories. For creators who use podcasting as a lead generation channel, having it inside the same platform as their courses and email list is genuinely convenient.

Where Kajabi Falls Short

The Pricing Gets Expensive Fast

Kajabi's pricing is structured around product limits and contact caps:

  • Kickstarter ($71/month billed annually): 1 product, 50 active members, 250 contacts, 50 email sends per month. Essentially a trial tier.
  • Basic ($119/month billed annually): 3 products, 1,000 active members, 10,000 contacts. This is the realistic starting point.
  • Growth ($159/month billed annually): 15 products, 10,000 active members, 25,000 contacts. Removes Kajabi branding.
  • Pro ($319/month billed annually): 100 products, 20,000 active members, 100,000 contacts. Full feature set.

A creator with 4 courses and 12,000 email subscribers is already pushed to the Growth plan at $159/month. Scale to 30,000 contacts or need more than 15 products and you are on Pro at $319/month. GoHighLevel Starter at $97/month has no contact limits, no product caps, and no member restrictions.

No Real CRM

Kajabi has contacts. It has tags. It has basic automations. What it does not have is a CRM in any meaningful sense. There is no visual pipeline. No deal stages. No lead scoring. No way to track where a prospect is in your sales process beyond "subscribed" and "purchased." For a creator selling a $47 ebook, this is fine. For a coach selling a $5,000 program that requires a discovery call, a proposal, and follow-up, the absence of pipeline management is a real gap.

No SMS Marketing

Kajabi is email only. No SMS. No two-way texting. No missed call text-back. In 2026, SMS open rates still hover around 98% compared to 20-25% for email. For launch sequences, webinar reminders, and cart abandonment recovery, SMS is not a nice-to-have — it is revenue you are leaving behind. GoHighLevel includes SMS natively with two-way conversations.

No Appointment Scheduling

If you sell high-ticket coaching or consulting, you need a way for prospects to book calls. Kajabi does not include scheduling. You need Calendly ($12-$20/month) or a similar tool, plus an integration to pass data back. GoHighLevel has built-in scheduling with round-robin, calendar sync, automated reminders, and no-show follow-up workflows.

Limited Automation Depth

Kajabi's automations cover the basics: when someone purchases, tag them and send a sequence. When they fill out a form, add them to a pipeline (Kajabi calls them "pipelines" but they are really just visual email sequences). The automation engine does not support conditional branching, multi-channel triggers (SMS plus email plus wait conditions), webhook actions, or the kind of if/then logic that GoHighLevel's workflow builder handles natively.

What GoHighLevel Brings to the Table

GoHighLevel approaches the course creator market from the opposite direction. It started as an agency and service business platform and expanded into courses, memberships, and communities. The result is a tool that is weaker on content delivery polish but dramatically stronger on everything that surrounds it.

Full CRM and Pipeline Management. Visual pipelines with custom stages, deal values, and automated stage transitions. Track every lead from first touch to closed deal. For coaches and consultants selling high-ticket programs through discovery calls, this is not optional — it is how you manage revenue.

SMS Marketing and Two-Way Texting. Send broadcast SMS campaigns, automated text sequences, and one-on-one conversations from the same platform. Missed call text-back automatically sends a text when you cannot answer the phone. For course creators running live launches, SMS reminders for webinars and cart close deadlines consistently outperform email alone.

Appointment Scheduling. Built-in calendars with round-robin assignment, buffer times, custom availability, and automated confirmations plus reminders via email and SMS. No Calendly subscription. No integration headaches.

Advanced Workflow Automation. GoHighLevel's workflow builder supports multi-channel sequences (email, then SMS, then voicemail drop, then wait, then conditional branch based on link click), if/else logic, webhook triggers, and custom event triggers. Build a workflow that sends an email when someone watches 80% of your webinar replay, follows up with SMS 24 hours later if they have not purchased, and assigns a task to your sales rep if they click the pricing link. Kajabi cannot do this.

Funnel Builder with Split Testing. Drag-and-drop page builder with A/B testing, countdown timers, and multi-step funnels. Build opt-in pages, webinar registration pages, sales pages, checkout pages, and thank-you pages without a separate tool.

Membership and Course Hosting. GHL added course and membership areas that cover the fundamentals: modules, lessons, video hosting, drip schedules, and gated access. The builder is functional and continues to improve. It is not as polished as Kajabi's student experience, but it gets the job done for most course formats.

Flat Pricing with No Caps. $97/month for Starter. Unlimited contacts. Unlimited courses. Unlimited members. Unlimited users. No penalty for growth.

Where GoHighLevel Will Not Match Kajabi

Honesty matters here. GoHighLevel has genuine gaps compared to Kajabi for pure course businesses:

  • Course student experience. Kajabi's course interface is more polished, more intuitive for students, and communicates higher perceived value. If you are selling a premium course at $997 or more, the presentation quality of your learning environment matters. Kajabi delivers a better first impression.
  • Native assessments. Quizzes, surveys, and completion certificates are built into Kajabi. GHL does not offer these natively — you would need a workaround using forms or a third-party tool.
  • Community depth. Kajabi's community features are more mature, with challenges, events, and a dedicated mobile experience. GHL has community functionality but it is newer and less developed.
  • Podcast hosting. Kajabi hosts and distributes podcasts. GHL does not.
  • Content-first design philosophy. Everything in Kajabi is designed around the idea that content is the product. The entire UX assumes you are a creator. GHL's UX assumes you are running a service business that happens to also sell courses. This fundamental design orientation shows up in dozens of small ways.

Pricing Breakdown: The Real Numbers

Pricing is where the comparison gets stark. Kajabi's model charges you more as you grow. GoHighLevel's model stays flat.

Scenario 1: New course creator, 1 course, 2,000 subscribers.

Kajabi Basic: $119/month. GoHighLevel Starter: $97/month. The difference is small. At this stage, Kajabi's superior course experience arguably justifies the extra $22/month if courses are your sole business.

Scenario 2: Growing creator, 5 courses, 15,000 subscribers, coaching calls.

Kajabi Growth: $159/month. But you also need Calendly for booking calls ($16/month) and an SMS tool for launch sequences (roughly $30-$50/month). Total: $205-$225/month. GoHighLevel: $97/month with scheduling, SMS, and unlimited courses included.

Scenario 3: Established creator, 20 products, 50,000 subscribers, team of 3.

Kajabi Pro: $319/month. Plus Calendly ($16/month), an SMS platform ($75/month), and a proper CRM for high-ticket sales ($50-$100/month). Total: $460-$510/month. GoHighLevel: $97/month. Same team. Same contacts. No add-ons.

At scale, the difference is roughly $4,000-$5,000 per year. That is real money for a course business operating on digital product margins.

Course Delivery: A Deeper Look

This is the section where Kajabi genuinely earns its premium. Course delivery is not just about hosting video files — it is about the experience your students have when they log in, navigate your content, and complete your program.

Kajabi's course player is clean, distraction-free, and optimized for learning. Students see a sidebar navigation with modules and lessons, a progress bar, and clear completion indicators. Video playback is smooth with adjustable speed. The mobile experience works without compromises. When a student finishes a module, they can take a quiz, receive a certificate, and get automatically moved to the next section. For creators selling transformation — weight loss programs, professional certifications, business bootcamps — this structured experience reinforces the value of the product and improves completion rates.

GoHighLevel's membership area is functional. You can create courses with modules and lessons, upload or embed videos, add text and downloadable resources, and drip content on a schedule. The student interface gets the job done. But it was clearly built as an addition to a marketing platform rather than designed from the ground up as a learning environment. There are no native quizzes. No completion certificates. The visual design is simpler. For a $47 mini-course or a bonus training included with a service package, this is perfectly adequate. For a flagship $2,000 certification program where the learning experience is part of the product's value proposition, you will feel the difference.

Email Marketing: Both Capable, Different Strengths

Both platforms include email marketing. Kajabi offers broadcast emails, automated sequences, tagging based on behavior (page visits, purchases, email clicks), and visual email builders. It covers what most course creators need for launch sequences, nurture campaigns, and post-purchase onboarding.

GoHighLevel's email system offers similar functionality but adds two critical layers. First, it is part of a multi-channel system — a single workflow can send an email, wait for a response, then send an SMS, then create a task for your team if the contact does not engage. Second, it integrates with the CRM pipeline, so email behavior can automatically move contacts through deal stages. A prospect who clicks your pricing link in an email can be automatically moved to "Hot Lead" in your pipeline and assigned to a rep.

For pure course launches, both platforms handle email adequately. For businesses where email is one channel in a multi-touch sales process, GoHighLevel's integration with SMS, pipeline, and scheduling gives it a clear edge.

Checkout and Payment Processing

Kajabi's checkout experience is one of its strongest features. Clean, mobile-optimized checkout pages with order bumps, one-click upsells, coupon codes, free trials, and flexible payment plans. Kajabi processes payments through Stripe and PayPal, keeping the transaction inside the platform. The zero-friction path from checkout to course access — where the student account is created and the product is delivered instantly upon purchase — is a genuine conversion advantage.

GoHighLevel handles payments through Stripe integration with order forms that support payment plans and one-time purchases. The checkout experience is solid but less refined than Kajabi's. Order bumps and one-click post-purchase upsells are available but require more manual configuration. For high-ticket offers that close on a call rather than a checkout page, GHL's strength is the pipeline and CRM that tracks the deal — the checkout itself is just the final step.

Kajabi also handles affiliate management natively, letting you recruit and manage affiliates who promote your courses. GoHighLevel has affiliate management capabilities as well, though Kajabi's system is more mature for course-specific affiliate programs.

The CRM and Sales Process Gap

This is where the platforms diverge most dramatically, and it matters more than most course creators realize.

If you sell a $47 ebook or a $197 course, you probably do not need a CRM. Someone sees your ad, visits your sales page, and either buys or does not. The sales process is transactional. Kajabi handles this perfectly.

If you sell a $3,000 coaching program, the process looks different. A prospect opts in to a webinar. They attend (or watch the replay). They book a discovery call. Your team follows up. There is a proposal stage. There might be a second call. Eventually, they sign up or go cold — and you need a re-engagement sequence for cold leads. This is a pipeline. This requires deal stages, task assignments, follow-up automation, and visibility into where every prospect stands.

Kajabi has no answer for this. You can tag contacts and send email sequences, but you cannot visualize a pipeline, assign deal values, track conversion rates by stage, or automate task creation for your team. GoHighLevel was built for exactly this workflow. The visual pipeline, combined with SMS follow-up, appointment scheduling, and multi-channel automation, means you can manage a high-ticket sales process without bolting on three other tools.

Who Should Choose What

Choose Kajabi If

  • Your entire business is selling courses and digital products. No service component. No coaching calls that need scheduling. No outbound sales. Just great content, a checkout page, and an email list. Kajabi's course delivery experience is unmatched for this model.
  • Premium student experience is part of your brand. If you sell high-ticket courses where the learning environment needs to feel like a $2,000 product, Kajabi's interface does the heavy lifting.
  • Community engagement drives retention. For membership businesses where discussion, challenges, and events keep members paying month after month, Kajabi's community tools are more developed.
  • You want podcasting in the same platform. Built-in podcast hosting and distribution is a genuine convenience that GoHighLevel does not offer.
  • You are non-technical and want the simplest path. Kajabi's UX is designed for creators, not marketers. The learning curve is gentler for someone whose primary skill is teaching, not funnel building.

Choose GoHighLevel If

  • You sell courses AND services. Coaching, consulting, agency work, or any model where courses are one revenue stream alongside done-for-you work. GHL handles both sides without separate tools.
  • High-ticket sales require a pipeline. Discovery calls, proposals, follow-up sequences, deal stages — GHL's CRM was built for this. Kajabi was not.
  • SMS is part of your marketing. Launch sequences, webinar reminders, cart abandonment texts, appointment confirmations. Kajabi has no SMS capability whatsoever.
  • You need appointment scheduling. Coaching calls, strategy sessions, onboarding calls — built-in scheduling with automated reminders eliminates Calendly from your stack.
  • Price matters at scale. As your contact list and product catalog grow, Kajabi's pricing scales up while GHL stays at $97/month. At 50,000 contacts, the difference is $200+ per month.
  • You run an agency. GHL's white-label SaaS Pro plan lets you rebrand the entire platform and resell it to your own clients. This is a revenue stream Kajabi cannot offer.

The Bottom Line

Kajabi and GoHighLevel are both excellent platforms built for fundamentally different business models. Choosing between them is not about which is "better" — it is about which matches the way you actually make money.

If your business is built entirely around creating and selling courses, memberships, and digital products — and the learning experience is part of your brand — Kajabi is purpose-built for you. Its course delivery, checkout flow, community tools, and creator-first design philosophy make it the strongest pure course platform available. You pay more, and you get a more polished product experience in return.

If your business involves coaching calls, service delivery, outbound sales, appointment scheduling, or multi-channel marketing — even if courses are part of the mix — GoHighLevel gives you dramatically more capability for less money. CRM, SMS, scheduling, pipeline, automation, and course hosting in one platform for $97/month. No contact caps. No product limits. No stack of add-ons.

Most founders reading this comparison are not running a pure course business. They are running a hybrid — courses plus coaching, content plus consulting, digital products plus done-for-you services. For that reality, GoHighLevel is the more complete platform at a price that does not punish you for growing.
