The 14-Day GoHighLevel Free Trial Action Plan

By day 14, you will have built enough to make a confident yes-or-no decision about whether GHL belongs in your business.

Days 1-2: CRM Setup and Contact Import

Goal: Get your existing contacts into GHL so the rest of the trial runs on real data.

Day 1: Export contacts from your current CRM or spreadsheet as CSV. In GHL, go to Contacts → Import and map your columns (name, email, phone, tags). Create custom fields first if you have specialized data (lead source, service type, deal value). Then build your tag structure: lead source tags (referral, website, cold-outreach), status tags (new-lead, contacted, booked-call, client), and service tags.

Day 2: Set up your business profile (logo, sender info, timezone). Connect your Google or Outlook calendar for availability sync. Create 2-3 Smart Lists — saved filters like "Hot leads" (new-lead tag, last 30 days) and "Cold leads to reactivate" (no activity in 60+ days). Explore a few contact records to see the unified conversation timeline — every text, email, call, and form submission in one place.

If you need prospects fast, Instantly is a cold email platform that pairs well with GHL. Run cold outreach campaigns in Instantly, then route interested replies into your GHL CRM and pipeline for follow-up and booking.

Days 3-4: Build Your First Funnel

Goal: Create a working lead capture page.

Day 3: Go to Sites → Funnels → Create New Funnel. Build a two-step funnel: a landing page with a lead capture form (first name, email, phone, one qualifying question), and a thank-you page. GHL natively connects form submissions to the CRM — no Zapier needed.

Day 4: Customize design (logo, brand colors, compelling copy), set up a custom domain (Settings → Domains), and test end-to-end: submit the form, confirm the contact appears in your CRM with correct tags. If you have time, build a booking page you can link from your email signature and social profiles.

Days 5-6: Set Up Your Booking Calendar

Goal: Replace Calendly with GHL's built-in booking system.

Day 5: Go to Calendars → Create Calendar. Configure a discovery call calendar with your availability, 15-minute buffer between appointments, 24-hour minimum scheduling notice, and pre-call questions. Connect Google or Outlook for two-way sync to prevent double-bookings. See our GHL calendar setup guide for the full walkthrough.

Day 6: Set up automated reminders (confirmation email, 24-hour SMS reminder, 1-hour SMS reminder). Embed the booking calendar on your funnel's thank-you page so the flow is: form submission → thank-you → book a call. Test by booking a test appointment and verifying reminders fire correctly.

Days 7-8: Create Your First Automation Workflow

Goal: Build automated follow-up that engages leads without you touching anything.

Day 7: Go to Automation → Workflows → Create Workflow. Build a new lead follow-up: trigger on Form Submitted, then (1) send instant SMS acknowledging their inquiry, (2) send welcome email with booking link, (3) add "new-lead" tag, (4) create pipeline opportunity. Add a 1-day wait, then an If/Else checking for "booked-call" tag — if booked, end; if not, send follow-up SMS. Continue the pattern over 6 days for 5 total touchpoints across email and SMS. See our workflow automation guide for complete templates.

Day 8: Build a missed call text-back workflow: trigger on Missed Call → wait 1 minute → send SMS ("Sorry I missed your call, what can I help with?") → internal notification. Test both workflows by enrolling yourself as a test contact. Publish both (toggle Draft → Published).

Days 9-10: Pipeline and Integrations

Goal: Build your sales pipeline and connect GHL to your other tools.

Day 9: Go to Opportunities → Pipelines → Create Pipeline with five stages: New Lead → Contacted → Call Booked → Proposal Sent → Won/Lost. Update your Day 7 workflow to auto-create opportunities. Add stage-change automations: calendar booking moves to Call Booked, "proposal-sent" tag moves to Proposal Sent.

Day 10: Connect integrations (Google, Facebook, Stripe, Zoom, QuickBooks — whatever you use). Then test the entire flow end-to-end: form submission → contact created → workflow fires → opportunity appears in pipeline → book test call → reminders send → pipeline stage updates. If all of this works, you have a complete client acquisition system.

Days 11-12: Run a Small Campaign

Goal: Send real traffic through your system.

Day 11: Pick one lead source: share your funnel on social media, add it to your email signature, send it to 10-20 warm contacts, or run a small $20-50 ad. If you have cold leads from your Day 1 import, send a reactivation text to 20-30 of them. Watch your dashboard as real leads flow through the system you built in 10 days.

Day 12: Follow up personally with responders using GHL's unified inbox. Check workflow execution logs (Automation → Workflows → Execution Logs) to verify automations fired correctly. Review your pipeline — are stages updating as expected?

Days 13-14: Evaluate and Decide

Goal: Make a data-driven decision.

Day 13: Review reporting: new contacts, funnel conversion rate, booking rate, email metrics, pipeline conversions. Calculate what GHL replaces — add up every tool you currently pay for that GHL covers (CRM, email, funnels, calendar, SMS, automation). For most solo founders, that is $200-500/month vs. GHL's $97/month.

Day 14: Decide. If continuing, everything you built is your production system. If cancelling, go to Settings → Billing → Cancel. If on the fence: "Did GHL handle tasks that were eating my time or falling through cracks?" If yes, that is worth $97/month.

Common GoHighLevel Free Trial Mistakes to Avoid

The same mistakes come up every time. Avoid these and you will get far more value from your 14 days.

Spending the first week watching tutorials instead of building. YouTube is a trap during a free trial. You learn GHL by building inside GHL, not by watching someone else's screen. Open the platform in one tab and this guide in another. Build first, watch tutorials only when stuck on a specific feature.

Building everything before testing anything. Founders spend 12 days perfecting a system, then realize on day 13 they never sent traffic through it. Build the minimum viable version of each component, then test with real data. A simple funnel that actually captures leads beats a polished one that never gets published.

Skipping phone and email setup on Day 1. SMS and email are core to the GHL experience. If you skip Twilio/LC Phone setup and email domain authentication, you are evaluating the platform without its two most powerful channels. Thirty minutes on Day 1 prevents this.

Not installing the mobile app. GHL's mobile app (LeadConnector) puts your CRM, conversations, and pipeline in your pocket. Replying to a lead's text from your phone — without giving out your personal number — is one of GHL's most underrated features for solo founders.

Not importing real contacts. An empty CRM teaches you nothing. Import your actual contact list on Day 1. Real names and real data make the evaluation meaningful.

Forgetting to cancel if it is not right. Set a calendar reminder for day 13. The charge hits on day 15 if you forget. Do this the moment you sign up.

Why 14 Days Is Enough to Decide

You do not need to master every feature to evaluate the platform. You need to answer three questions:

  1. Can GHL replace what I am paying for? By Day 6, you will have a funnel (replaces ClickFunnels), a booking calendar (replaces Calendly), and a working CRM (replaces HubSpot or your spreadsheet). If those work, the consolidation alone justifies the subscription.
  2. Does automation save me time? By Day 8, you will have live workflows handling lead follow-up and missed calls. Track how many automated messages go out in Days 9-14. If the number exceeds ten, automation is earning its keep.
  3. Does one dashboard feel better than juggling tools? By Day 10, everything is connected. Pay attention to the experience of managing it all from one place. If it feels like relief, stay. If it feels like another tool to manage, it might not be your fit.
